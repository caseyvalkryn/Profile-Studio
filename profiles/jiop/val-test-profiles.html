Common geometry

  • Camera line. CAM-1 and CAM-2 fixed at 80°, CAM-3 at 2× · 45.5°, CAM-4 at 5× · 17.6°, 10 m apart across the axis, lens height 2.5 m. Each camera is yawed to the axis point 100 m before the camera line and tilted up by the angle in the table; survey and record azimuth and tilt per camera. Each position is surveyed on its own and VAL computes range per camera from its own position (plan section 6). The sectors show planning reach from the Appendix A.1 estimate, not measured range. The ladder measures it.
  • Axis. One straight line from the launch point at the NW end to the camera line, heading 142°. Every approach flies it head-on, so heading and aspect hold as plan section 7 step 5 requires.
  • Nodes. GCS-1 behind the launch point logs the aircraft. REC-1 behind the cameras records the four streams. Both carry a truth station for VAL.

Profile 1 · JIOP-1 · flown first

Range Ladder · Mavic 3 by distance band

Why first. It sets the distance bands the other three profiles are read against. Five hovers of 30 s give every camera one equal observation interval per band, so detection probability per band comes from equal exposure.

What is tested

  • Factors: distance band, five levels, one hover at each (450, 350, 250, 150, 75 m from the camera line); camera configuration, four levels, observed in parallel.
  • Responses: outcome per band, per camera, per run: possible, confirmed, identified, or none, with the FOV-limited flag. Range error between the VAL range and the GCS range.

Run plan

RunPathHoverTransitAlt
L1 to L3Launch → H1 450 m → H2 → H3 → H4 → H5 75 m, inbound, head-on30 s each5 m/s40 m AGL

Three replicates, about 6 min each. Inbound order on every run keeps the aspect head-on; the three replicates carry the light drift. Add a fourth run if two bands disagree between runs. Planning first cue: CAM-4 at ≈470 m, CAM-3 at ≈405 m, CAM-1 and CAM-2 at ≈196 m.

VAL output

  • Detection matrix: band × camera × run, each cell linked to the footage interval and the GCS position.
  • Detection probability per band with its confidence interval. Missing frames and frame-time synchronization error per run (C.2).

Profile 2 · JIOP-2 · flown second

One-Way Attack · Tanq FPV direct run

Why. This is the paper's core method. One aircraft, one controlled approach, three detection events per camera, each tied to a time, a range, and a frame. The terminal dive adds the warning-time measure and tests the elevation limit of the zoomed cameras.

What is tested

  • Events: E1 first possible visual cue, E2 first confirmed detection, E3 first identifiable frame (section 7, step 6). Confirmed means the reviewer locates the same moving airborne target across consecutive frames without cueing from the flight log (section 8).
  • Responses: ground and slant range at each event, per camera, from each camera's surveyed position. Warning time from first cue to the aim point. Timing uncertainty and recording gaps.

Run plan

RunPathSpeedAltEnd
A1 to A3Launch → 100 m short of the camera line, then a 41° dive to the aim point 60 m short15 m/s40 m AGL → 5 mHold 5 s at the aim point

Three replicates, about 36 s from launch to the aim point. No payload, no flight toward people. At 15 m/s, 1 s of clock offset is 15 m of range (section 8), so the clock record decides the range uncertainty here.

Side view · elevation limits

The run crosses each camera's upper elevation limit at the marked distance, with the tilt in the camera table and a 4:3 sensor. Inside it the camera cannot see the aircraft, so a loss there is FOV-limited, not an optical limit (section 6). On a 16:9 sensor the limits move out to 193 m (CAM-4), 96 m (CAM-3) and 53 m (CAM-1, CAM-2). The 80° cameras hold the target to the aim point either way.

VAL output

  • Aircraft track and detection timeline on one clock. Each event links to its video frame and its calculated range, with the clock-offset uncertainty as a ± value.
  • Each event row carries the C.1 fields: run and camera ID, the three event times, frame index and still, drone and camera E/N/U, ground and slant range, time and range uncertainty, FOV-limited and occluded flags, reviewer.

Profile 3 · JIOP-4 · flown third

Controlled Repeat · optical zoom 1× against 5×

Why. One factor changes, nothing else. CAM-3 and CAM-4 fly the same Mavic 3 approach at 1× and then at 5×. CAM-1 and CAM-2 stay at 80° as the control, so a change in light shows in the control cameras and not in the factor.

What is tested

  • Factor: true optical zoom on the two varifocal cameras, two levels, 1× (80°) and 5× (17.6°). The paper names this change in C.3 item 4 and Phase 4. Defined background can replace zoom with the same profile.
  • Responses: first possible, confirmed, and identified range per camera per run. The difference between levels against the spread within a level. FOV loss at the near end at 5×.

Run plan

R1, R3, R51×Launch → 50 m short of the camera line, hold 10 s8 m/s40 m AGL

Six runs, levels alternated so sky and sun changes do not confound the factor. Re-zoom and re-snapshot CAM-3 and CAM-4 between runs. The spread between CAM-1 to CAM-4 at the 1× level is the repeatability floor.

  • Side-by-side runs with their settings, conditions, detection events, and evidence.
  • First confirmed range distribution per level and camera, median and percentiles (C.2), with the between-level difference and the within-level spread on one plot.

Profile 4 · JIOP-3 · flown last

Swarm · seven aircraft with crossings

Why last. It needs every aircraft in the inventory and the most coordination. The single-aircraft ranges from Profiles 1 to 3 say what each aircraft should produce alone, so missed and false observations in the multi-aircraft case stand out.

What is tested

  • Per aircraft: E1, E2, E3 events and ranges as in Profile 2, with each aircraft's GCS log recorded separately.
  • Multi-aircraft: missed observations, false detections, and correct association of each observation with one aircraft by time and bearing against the logs. The two crossings of T3 and T4, 5 m apart in height, are the closest spacing and test resolution.

Data preserved for every run

  • Four camera recordings, RTSP main stream, continuous, native resolution, 25 fps, MP4 segments with timestamps
  • Camera configuration snapshot per run per camera (Appendix B.1 JSON): resolution, fps, FOV, zoom, focus, exposure, time offset
  • Camera survey record: WGS84 position, lens height, azimuth, tilt, roll, installation photo
  • Aircraft GCS telemetry log at the maximum rate: GPS time, latitude, longitude, altitude, accuracy
  • ATOS tag track for the same aircraft (VAL fused), for comparison with the GCS log
  • Clock synchronization record: PC, camera, video and telemetry offsets with the measured uncertainty
  • Detection event table per camera: first possible, first confirmed, first identified; frame index, UTC, still image, reviewer
  • Computed ranges per event: aircraft and camera ENU positions, ground range, slant range, uncertainty, FOV-limited and occluded flags
  • Environmental record per run: sun position, sky background, cloud, visibility, wind, obstructions
  • Target-absent recording interval per run (120 s before launch) for the false-detection count
